## 1. Concept directions (five)

The visual language is still open, so here are five options rather than one assumed answer.

1. **Receipts.** The site already argues "Receipts, not testimonials" ([hasan.md:245](../../hasan.md)). The reel is a hand passing real documents across warm paper: a Play listing with 1,063 ratings, a store upload screen, a case-study row, a commit graph. Every claim is shown *as an artifact*, never as a boast. Cost: cheapest to verify — each receipt is a screenshot of something that exists.
2. **The cable.** Six years as one physical line running left-to-right; products hang off it at their dates. Elegant, but it makes every beat the same composition — which atelier mode explicitly forbids at scene level.
3. **Two surfaces.** Split screen: the code on the left, the thing it became on the right. Strong for the app clips, weak for a career reel — it can only show one product at a time.
4. **Dubai desk.** Geography as the hook: an engineer in Dubai shipping into New Zealand, Saudi Arabia, and the Gulf. True and distinctive, but it foregrounds location over evidence and reads as mood copy.
5. **One minute after submit.** Each beat is the moment after pressing *Submit for Review*. Memorable conceit, but the timing is fictional — we can't show what we didn't record.

**Recommendation: 1 (Receipts), with 3 borrowed for exactly one beat.** It is the only direction whose every frame is defensible, and it extends a voice the site already speaks instead of inventing one for the video.

## 4. Composition mode: atelier

Marketing and hero work default to **atelier** ([skills/meta/bespoke-composition.md](file:///Users/macbookm1pro/tools/OpenMontage/skills/meta/bespoke-composition.md)): the stock scene catalog, registry blocks-as-scenes, and fixtures are off-limits. Each scene gets its own primary visual subject; the signature device appears in one or at most two beats.

Art direction comes from the real identity, not a stock playbook — measured: `styles/clean-professional.yaml` ships `#2563EB` blue and Inter, which is not this brand.


## 10. Approval policy — pick one

OpenMontage treats gates as binding and per-gate: an early "go ahead" does **not** cover later gates unless full-run pre-authorization is recorded as a `decision_log` entry with `category: "approval_policy"`.

- **A. Per-gate (default).** ~16 stop-and-confirm moments across three projects. Safest, slowest, most turns spent on you reading tables.
- **B. Full-run pre-authorization.** One entry logged per project; I proceed through `edit`/`compose` automatically and still stop at `assets` (filmstrip review) and `publish` (files before posting). Recommended: the gates that actually matter here are the ones where you look at real pixels and real numbers.

Either way I stop before any paid or consequential call — there are none — and before anything goes live.

## 11. Risks and limits (stated up front)

1. **2.8 GB free RAM of 16 GB, 22.1 GB disk free.** Headless-Chrome frame renders are memory-bound. Mitigation: render per-scene rather than one 45s timeline, keep composites ≤1080p, and free the browser before each compose. If a render OOMs, that's a runtime swap proposal to you — never a silent substitution.
2. **`clip-factory` is beta.** Fallback named in §2.
3. **Live Play ratings can't be curl'd.** They're read in Chrome at the `assets` stage; if the number moved, the caption moves with it and I tell you.
4. **`hasan.md` says 15 app profiles; live CMS says 21.** Reconcile before the count appears on screen (§8).
5. **OpenMontage is AGPL-3.0 and lives outside this repo** at `/Users/macbookm1pro/tools/OpenMontage`. Nothing from it is vendored into `portfolio/`; only the rendered files and this plan land in the repo. No license exposure to the site's code.
6. **Playwright extension follows your front-most tab.** Every browser action resolves its page by URL inside the same evaluate, and I do not touch your Hostinger or Play Console tabs.
7. **FFmpeg is 9.0.2.** Loudness figures are comparable only against the same version.
8. **`remotion-composer` reports 11 npm vulnerabilities (2 moderate, 9 high)** from `make setup`. Local dev-time build tool, not deployed anywhere; I'll list the advisory paths before the first Remotion render rather than shrugging at them.
